The serviceable line · Part two

The shape of the serviceable line How far does your customer's AI get without you?

The five layers are not the same size for every offering. A bag of rice has almost nothing to connect or fix. A medical device has a hospital committee to convince and technicians to train. A software platform has all five, in depth. So the line has a shape, and so does the work around it.

Published 3 October 2026 · 7 minute read

Every offering asks something of the person buying it. Some ask almost nothing. Some ask a great deal, before the purchase and long after it.

The serviceable line is how far your customers, and increasingly their AI, get with your offering on their own.

That distance only means something against how much there is to get through. So before asking where your line sits, ask how big your surface is.

How to read the shape

A surface, a line and a gap

Each spoke is one of the five layers: Findable, Selectable, Integrable, Operable and Fixable. Three things sit on them.

The surface
Everything someone has to work out or do to get value from your offering, on each layer: where to find it, how it compares, what it has to connect to, how to run it, and what to do when it goes wrong. A long spoke means a lot of work on that layer. No spoke means the layer does not exist for this offering.
The line
How much of that surface your customers, and increasingly their AI, get through on their own.
Past the line
  • One of your people steps in. This is seen and costed: it is your cost to serve.
  • The customer is lost. This ranges from invisible (left out of an AI answer, never entered the funnel), to seen as a number without its cause (a trial that did not convert, churn, a deal that went small), to seen and explained, which happens only when a person was there.

On the drawings, grey is the gap.

Moving the line out shrinks the gap from the inside. How much there is to shrink depends on the offering.

Six offerings

Six offerings, six shapes

These shapes are illustrative. They are drawn from how each kind of offering is typically bought and used, not from measurement. Read the outline, not the numbers. Teal marks work someone else carries, such as a partner, a dealer or a service company; more on that below.

What the shapes show

What the outlines make plain

Industry is the wrong cut

The question is the shape of the offering, not the sector it sits in. A cordless drill shares more layers with a software product than with a bag of rice, though the drill and the rice are both consumer goods.

A missing layer is not a failing score

The rice is not bad at Fixable. It has nothing to fix. Judge each offering only against the surface it has, or every simple product looks like it is failing.

Findable stays a battleground

For anything that is not a commodity, Findable is a contest for a few places, and like search before it, it is never settled, because every competitor is fighting for the same answer. An AI answer usually names a handful of options, not a page of ten links.

Selectable is where the newest silent loss happens

When price, limits and fit criteria are missing, the AI compares you against a smaller version of yourself, or fills the gap with someone else's numbers. The longer the Selectable spoke, the more that costs.

In regulated offerings, the rules hold the line in

On a payments platform or a medical device, some layers stay short whatever the customer's AI can do, because a regulator, a committee or the cost of error requires a person. That is the buyer side of the two variables: gated by permission, accountability, regulation and the cost of error, not by what the models can do.

A permutation

When someone else carries the work

Partnerships, after-sales services, agencies, dealers, integrators and repair sites are not a separate model. They are a permutation of this one: part of the surface past your line is carried by someone who is neither you nor your customer. On the drawings, that is the teal.

That work is partly seen. It gets done, but you are leaking margin, control and the record the next buyer's AI reads. Sometimes nobody asked them to carry it: if you do not publish the answer, the AI gets it elsewhere, such as price from review sites, compatibility from forums, or the repair from a repair site. iFixit already offers an AI repair assistant for products whose makers left that layer empty.

And the AI doing that work is not always the buyer's. On a medical device, much of the fixing is done by a distributor's or the hospital's own technician, increasingly with their own AI. The question does not change: how far does the work get before one of your people has to step in?

Bring it in-house

When your offering carries more on its own, some of the work a middleman did for a fee comes back to you, and so does the margin.

Or make them better at it

The documentation, interfaces and recovery paths that move your line also make your partners faster. A partner whose work gets easier sells and services more of you.

Which move fits is a channel decision. Either way, it starts with the same work: an offering legible enough to be carried without you.

One offering, two buyers

The surface belongs to the buyer, too

The same software, sold to a small business and to an enterprise, has two different surfaces. The enterprise adds a security review, procurement and connections to many systems. At the small business, the person evaluating it is often the person buying it and the person setting it up, in the same afternoon.

So the line is set per buyer segment, not per company. That is also why the two variables behind it are read per buyer, not per industry.

Where the line sits now depends on two things that multiply: how far your buyers have gone in handing this work to AI, and how legible your offering is to a machine.

How it plays out

Both edges are moving

This section is forecast, not evidence.

The surfaces grow

Goods are becoming software-defined. The drill gets an app, the appliance gets firmware, the car gets updates. Each one adds Integrable, Operable and Fixable surface to something that had little.

So the gap can grow while the line moves out

Whether your gap shrinks depends on which edge moves faster: the line, as customers and their AI get further alone, or the surface, as the offering asks more of them.

The layers move at different speeds

Findable stays contested. Selectable is where the losses are moving now. On Integrable and Fixable, consumers already ask their AI the questions before they buy, but they do not hand it the doing, so the line moves through content first: compatibility lists, manuals and parts lists. Operable moves last, because it is gated by permission, accountability and the cost of error, not by what the models can do.

Regulation is publishing some surfaces by force

From 31 July 2026, the EU Right to Repair Directive requires producers of covered products, such as washing machines, refrigerators and phones, to offer repair beyond the guarantee, with repair information and spare parts to match. It was written as sustainability law. It also builds part of the record a customer's AI reads.

Use it

Draw your own shape

  1. For each layer, list what a buyer has to work out or do with your offering. If the list is empty, the layer does not exist for you. That is your surface.
  2. For each item, ask whether your customers, or their AI, can get through it from what you publish, without a person. That is your line.
  3. If partners, dealers, service companies or third-party sites carry part of it, mark that too.
  4. What is left is your gap: the work your people carry, and the customers you lose, often without seeing why.
  5. Draw it once per buyer segment, not once per company.

Scoring each layer

Use the same scale for the surface, the line and what others carry, so two people draw the same offering the same way.

  1. 0 Nothing to do. The layer does not exist for this offering.
  2. 1 A few facts to look up.
  3. 2 A procedure to follow.
  4. 3 A system to configure and keep running.

Compare shapes spoke by spoke, never by area. Area grows with the square of each value and changes with the order of the spokes, so two shapes of the same size can mean very different things.
