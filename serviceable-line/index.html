The serviceable line What can your customer's AI do without you?

Customers are beginning to hand more of the work of finding, comparing, connecting, running and fixing products to AI. What an AI can do without you depends on two things: what the customer is willing to delegate, and how legible and operable your product is to that AI. That is your serviceable line.

Published 29 August 2026 · Updated 26 September 2026 · 6 minute read

Customers have been getting further on their own for a decade: finding you, comparing you, connecting you, running the product, fixing it when it breaks. What is changing is who is doing it. Increasingly it is a machine.

The serviceable line is the share of the market, and of each customer's journey, you can win without adding people: the work a customer's AI can complete with your product before anyone from your company has to step in.

It is not another market-size formula. The word serviceable is deliberate: as in serviceable available market, it asks what demand a company can actually reach under current constraints. Here the unit is both the market and the customer journey.

Both sales-led and product-led motions are losing surface area to this silent part of the journey. The company does not own the machine doing the work. It often cannot see the interaction. And no single leader owns whether the machine's picture of the company matches what the company actually is.

The two variables behind the variance

The serviceable line is the output of two independent variables. The first is how far the customer's own AI adoption has got in that industry, gated by permission, accountability, regulation and the cost of error, not by model capability. The second is how legible and integrable the product is to a machine: whether an outsider's agent can find it, compare it, connect it, run it and recover it without a person.

They multiply, they do not add. A perfectly legible product sold into a market whose buyers do not yet delegate has a short line anyway. A market full of delegating buyers does nothing for a vendor a machine cannot read. That is why the line sits in a different place in developer tooling than in hospital procurement, and why the same vendor's line differs by segment inside one company.

One of those you do not control, and it is rising anyway. The other is a decision you have already made, deliberately or not.

The diagnostic

Five questions the customer's AI keeps asking

These are not stages in a funnel. The machine can consult any of them before or after money moves, and a weak answer at one point contaminates the others.

  1. 01

    Can it find you at all?

  2. 02

    Can it compare you honestly?

  3. 03

    Can it work out how you connect?

  4. 04

    Can it run the work?

  5. 05

    Can it recover when something breaks?

How to start measuring the line

The questions become useful when each has an observable test and a commercial signal. The point is not one total score. It is to locate where the work stops and what that stop costs.

Five places the job can stop

The silent losses

The funnel cannot report what never entered it

Omission

A capability is real but poorly described. The buyer's AI compares you against a smaller version of yourself, and the company never knows the capability was missing from the decision.

Disillusion

The buyer arrives with an implementation plan assembled from public material. The plan is wrong, so activation stalls and the failure becomes evidence for the next buyer.

The vacancy

Five answers, five teams, no owner

How a company is represented to machines is split across marketing, product, pricing, partnerships, documentation, and support. Each team may be doing its part well, while nobody owns whether the answers add up across the full customer journey.

This is a commercial job because the failure appears as lost consideration, lower conversion, slow activation, higher cost to serve, and quiet churn. The owner needs the authority to coordinate the whole system and the discipline to measure it end to end.

The current conversation

Where the serviceable line fits

Several active debates describe parts of the same shift. The serviceable line connects them across the customer journey and separates the force the market controls from the part a company can change.

AI visibility, AEO and GEO

Answer-engine optimization (AEO) and generative-engine optimization (GEO) ask whether AI can find, understand and cite a company. That is the first question. The serviceable line continues through comparison, connection, operation and recovery.

Agentic commerce and agentic buying

These describe people and companies delegating discovery, evaluation, purchasing and service to AI agents. That is the demand-side force moving the line, unevenly by industry and task.

AI agent readiness and machine legibility

These ask whether a vendor publishes accurate information, interfaces, permissions and recovery paths that an outside agent can use. This is the half of the line the company controls.

Cost to serve and human escalation

These make the line visible in the economics: where the journey still requires a person, how often an agent escalates, and what that does to activation, margin and churn.

Service-as-software and outcome-based pricing

These are consequences of the line moving. When work becomes autonomous enough and its result attributable enough, companies can sell completed work rather than seats or access.

To be tested

How fast each industry moves

The mechanism is proven in the human version. What is unproven is how quickly buyers in each industry hand this work to AI, which sets how far the line can move.

Current market signals

  • Stripe's agentic-commerce primer describes agents researching, comparing, provisioning and buying on behalf of people and businesses. It supports the demand-side shift, not the full five-question framework.
  • McKinsey's agentic-commerce automation curve separates what agents can do from what people will delegate, and shows why adoption varies by task and industry. It supports the first of the two variables.
  • Sierra's outcome-based pricing work ties autonomous, attributable work to a different commercial model. That is a consequence of the line moving, not evidence that every market has moved.
